Inside Tamil Nadu

Tamil Nadu lies in the Kaveri delta & south, and the land runs coastal-plain. That single fact decides a lot: what can be grown, how towns are shaped, and what a visitor should pack. Chennai is the administrative centre, but the character of the state is set by the district towns rather than the capital — and that is where the markets, the coaching centres and most state-level hiring actually sit.

The languages in daily use are Tamil · English. That combination matters if you are moving here for a course or a job: the language of the classroom and the language of the workplace are often not the same, and state-level hiring tends to happen in the regional language first, with English alongside.

What Tamil Nadu grows and makes

What leaves Tamil Nadu for the rest of the country falls into two groups. The fields are built around rice, cotton, sugarcane, groundnut. Alongside them sit the trades and industries that carry the rest of the economy: textile mills, leather, engineering, cotton ginning.

Read together, those two lists are the honest answer to what kind of work exists here — and they are why the city guides below break employment down sector by sector instead of quoting one statewide number that would mean nothing in any particular town.

When to go

November – February is the working window for a visit. The delta cool — post-monsoon paddy, temple circuits and pleasant market days. Day-to-day weather follows the terrain, so coastal-plain is the honest way to judge what you are walking into: put outdoor plans in the middle of the day and the markets, food and festivals in the evening.

morning

meal

The lunch thali — rice, sambar, kuzhambu, poriyal, appalam and buttermilk on the leaf.

street
Bajji–sundal stalls

The evening line — mirchi bajji, chana sundal and kadalai at the bazaar corners.

sweets
Mysore pak and adhirasam

The halwai classics — ghee mysore pak, adhirasam and festival laddus.

Festival calendar

The year in Tamil Nadu runs to a rhythm worth planning around. These are the dates that decide when shops close, when trains and hotels fill, and when a visit is worth making at all.

January
Pongal

The four-day harvest festival — sugarcane, pongal pots on every doorstep and Mattu Pongal for the cattle.

March–April
Chithirai Thiruvizha

The temple new-year festival — car processions and the Meenakshi–Kallazhagar wedding lore.

September–October
Navaratri Golu

The doll festival — golu steps in homes, sundal evenings and temple music.

December
Margazhi season

The music-and-devotion month — early-morning temple bajans and classical kutcheris.

Best season
November – February

November – February is the window. The delta cool — post-monsoon paddy, temple circuits and pleasant market days. The terrain here is coastal-plain, so plan outdoor time for the middle of the day.
