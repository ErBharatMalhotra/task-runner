Inside Chhattisgarh

Chhattisgarh lies in the Mahanadi plain, and the land runs plain. That single fact decides a lot: what can be grown, how towns are shaped, and what a visitor should pack. Raipur is the administrative centre, but the character of the state is set by the district towns rather than the capital — and that is where the markets, the coaching centres and most state-level hiring actually sit.

The languages in daily use are Hindi · Chhattisgarhi. That combination matters if you are moving here for a course or a job: the language of the classroom and the language of the workplace are often not the same, and state-level hiring tends to happen in the regional language first, with English alongside.

What Chhattisgarh grows and makes

What leaves Chhattisgarh for the rest of the country falls into two groups. The fields are built around rice, maize, groundnut, cane. Alongside them sit the trades and industries that carry the rest of the economy: steel, power, rice milling, forest produce.

Read together, those two lists are the honest answer to what kind of work exists here — and they are why the city guides below break employment down sector by sector instead of quoting one statewide number that would mean nothing in any particular town.

When to go

October – March is the working window for a visit. The plain winter — post-harvest mandis, haat days and comfortable melas. Day-to-day weather follows the terrain, so plain is the honest way to judge what you are walking into: put outdoor plans in the middle of the day and the markets, food and festivals in the evening.

The local sweets — khurmura (date-sesame), raskadam and gud treats.

Festival calendar

The year in Chhattisgarh runs to a rhythm worth planning around. These are the dates that decide when shops close, when trains and hotels fill, and when a visit is worth making at all.

August
Pola

The bullock festival — cattle washed, garlanded and paraded through every mohalla of the plain.

August–September
Teeja

The monsoon women’s festival — neem swings, folk songs and gifts across Chhattisgarh.

Gaur–Gorba nights

Navratri’s Chhattisgarhi dance form — dhol-led gaur circles on every colony ground.
