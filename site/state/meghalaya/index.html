Inside Meghalaya

Meghalaya lies in the Khasi and Garo hills of the north-east, and the land runs Shillong sits around 1,496 m. That single fact decides a lot: what can be grown, how towns are shaped, and what a visitor should pack. Shillong is the administrative centre, but the character of the state is set by the district towns rather than the capital — and that is where the markets, the coaching centres and most state-level hiring actually sit.

The languages in daily use are Khasi · Garo · English · Hindi. That combination matters if you are moving here for a course or a job: the language of the classroom and the language of the workplace are often not the same, and state-level hiring tends to happen in the regional language first, with English alongside.

What Meghalaya grows and makes

What leaves Meghalaya for the rest of the country falls into two groups. The fields are built around rice, maize, tea, citrus. Alongside them sit the trades and industries that carry the rest of the economy: coal and limestone mining, cement, tea, forestry.

Read together, those two lists are the honest answer to what kind of work exists here — and they are why the city guides below break employment down sector by sector instead of quoting one statewide number that would mean nothing in any particular town.

What people eat in Meghalaya

Four plates cover most of what a visitor is actually served in Meghalaya.

main plate
Jadoh

The Khasi rice dish cooked with pork and aromatic leaf, eaten with green chilli and fish sauce.

breakfast
Puta and steam-up rice

The Meghalaya morning plate — puta, the steamed rice cake, or jadoh from the roadside shops.

street
Nakham bitchi

The Shillong street snack — pork cooked with fermented bamboo shoot, served as a skewer or a roll.

sweets
Rice cakes and local fruit

Rice-and-jaggery cakes from the hills, with the season's fruit alongside.

Festival calendar

The year in Meghalaya runs to a rhythm worth planning around. These are the dates that decide when shops close, when trains and hotels fill, and when a visit is worth making at all.

Shad Suk Mynsiem

The Khasi five-day autumn festival — archery, dance and the torch ceremony in Shillong.

Nongkrem

The Garo harvest thanksgiving, a week of dance and ritual held on the Garo hills.

The Garo winter dance festival — the year's largest dance gathering, at Siong Ail.
