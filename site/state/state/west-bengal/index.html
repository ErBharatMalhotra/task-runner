Inside West Bengal

West Bengal lies in the Bengal delta, and the land runs delta-plain. That single fact decides a lot: what can be grown, how towns are shaped, and what a visitor should pack. Kolkata is the administrative centre, but the character of the state is set by the district towns rather than the capital — and that is where the markets, the coaching centres and most state-level hiring actually sit.

The languages in daily use are Bengali · Hindi · English. That combination matters if you are moving here for a course or a job: the language of the classroom and the language of the workplace are often not the same, and state-level hiring tends to happen in the regional language first, with English alongside.

What West Bengal grows and makes

What leaves West Bengal for the rest of the country falls into two groups. The fields are built around rice, jute, potato, tea. Alongside them sit the trades and industries that carry the rest of the economy: jute mills, engineering, tea trade, fisheries.

Read together, those two lists are the honest answer to what kind of work exists here — and they are why the city guides below break employment down sector by sector instead of quoting one statewide number that would mean nothing in any particular town.

When to go

October – February is the working window for a visit. The delta winter — puja aftermath, nolen-gur season and comfortable days. Day-to-day weather follows the terrain, so delta-plain is the honest way to judge what you are walking into: put outdoor plans in the middle of the day and the markets, food and festivals in the evening.

What people eat in West Bengal

Four plates cover most of what a visitor is actually served in West Bengal.

morning
Luchi–aloor dom kitchens

The Bengali breakfast — puffed luchi with potato curry and chai at the cabin restaurants.

street
Phuchka & jhalmuri stalls

The street classics — phuchka with tamarind water, jhalmuri and ghugni at the crossing.

meal
Bhaat–machher thali

The daily plate — rice, dal, bhaja, machher jhol and a seasonal vegetable.

sweets
Rosogolla & mishti doi

The sweet-house legends — rosogolla, sandesh and mishti doi in clay pots.

Festival calendar

The year in West Bengal runs to a rhythm worth planning around. These are the dates that decide when shops close, when trains and hotels fill, and when a visit is worth making at all.

September–October
Durga Puja

The state’s great festival — pandals, dhak drums, anjali and the sindoor-khela bijoya.

October–November
Kali Puja & Diwali

The lamp-and-goddess night — kali pandals, phuljhuri crackers and prasad.

January
Saraswati Puja

The students’ festival — books blessed, kolboishakhi yellow and kul-chutney season.

March
Dol (Holi)

The colour festival — abir, dol-jatra processions and kirtan through the paras.
