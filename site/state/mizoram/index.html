Inside Mizoram

Mizoram lies in the north-east hills, and the land runs Aizawl sits around 1,100 m in the hills. That single fact decides a lot: what can be grown, how towns are shaped, and what a visitor should pack. Aizawl is the administrative centre, but the character of the state is set by the district towns rather than the capital — and that is where the markets, the coaching centres and most state-level hiring actually sit.

The languages in daily use are Mizo · English · Hindi. That combination matters if you are moving here for a course or a job: the language of the classroom and the language of the workplace are often not the same, and state-level hiring tends to happen in the regional language first, with English alongside.

What Mizoram grows and makes

What leaves Mizoram for the rest of the country falls into two groups. The fields are built around rice, maize, oilseeds, banana. Alongside them sit the trades and industries that carry the rest of the economy: handloom and puan weaving, horticulture, agriculture, services.

Read together, those two lists are the honest answer to what kind of work exists here — and they are why the city guides below break employment down sector by sector instead of quoting one statewide number that would mean nothing in any particular town.

What people eat in Mizoram

Four plates cover most of what a visitor is actually served in Mizoram.

main plate
Bai

The everyday Mizo plate — steamed rice with boiled vegetables, pork or chicken, chilli and coriander.

breakfast
Vawksa rep

The Mizo breakfast — sticky rice with fried pork and onion, wrapped and eaten on the move.

street
Sa-ad

The roadside bowls of Aizawl — rice, boiled egg and shredded pork, washed down with chai.

sweets
Chhangban

Steamed rice and pork buns made at home and shared at festivals and family puities.

Festival calendar

The year in Mizoram runs to a rhythm worth planning around. These are the dates that decide when shops close, when trains and hotels fill, and when a visit is worth making at all.

March
Chapchar Kut

The spring festival of Mizoram — bamboo dance and a thalfavang feast held across the state.

August–September
Mim Kut

The maize festival — the first harvest, celebrated with local maize dishes and community games.

December
Pawl Kut

The December harvest thanksgiving — the year's largest Mizo gathering, with dance and feast.
