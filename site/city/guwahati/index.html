Guwahati in one minute

Guwahati — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Guwahati faces north across the Brahmaputra from the old Kamrupa heartland — the city’s Nilachal hill carries the Kamakhya temple, one of the oldest Shakti peeths, where the Ambubachi mela still closes the temple for three days each monsoon. On the plains below, tanks and islands (Dighalipukhuri, Umananda in mid-river) predate the modern grid.

It is Assam’s largest city and commercial capital — the Brahmaputra’s south-bank workhorse. Tea moves through its auction centre, oil through the Noonmati refinery, students through Gauhati University and the IIT Guwahati campus, and nearly all overland traffic to Meghalaya, Arunachal and the rest of the Northeast passes through it. Guwahati Junction (GHY) anchors the Barak–Brahmaputra rail link and Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi airport (GAU) flies the metros ~20 km west.

FAQ

Why is Guwahati famous?

For the Kamakhya temple and Ambubachi mela, the Brahmaputra riverfront and Assam’s tea trade — and as the gateway city to all of Northeast India.

What is the population of Guwahati?

As per Census 2011, Guwahati city has 9,63,404 residents; the urban agglomeration is 9,68,549 and the district 12,63,844.

What are the places to visit there?

Kamakhya temple, Umananda island, Dighalipukhuri tank, Kalakshetra and Saraighat — with Hajo, Shillong and Kaziranga as trips.

How do I reach Guwahati?

Guwahati Junction (GHY) on the main line to the Northeast, Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi airport (GAU) ~20 km, and NH-27.
