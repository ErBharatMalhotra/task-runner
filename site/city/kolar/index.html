Kolār in one minute

Kolār — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Kolār, one of the oldest cities in southern Karnataka, served as the early capital of the Ganga Dynasty. The city is geographically defined by its dry, boulder-strewn landscape, dominated by the massive Kolar Hills (Shatasringa Parvata). Historically synonymous with the legendary Kolar Gold Fields located in the wider district, the city itself functions as a vital administrative and trade hub connecting Bengaluru with Andhra Pradesh.

Modern Kolār has transitioned from its golden era to become a major hub for agriculture and dairy. It is home to KOMUL, one of the largest milk cooperatives in India, and is a principal supplier of tomatoes and silk to regional markets. The city is also famous for its ancient stone temples, particularly the Kolaramma Temple and the Someshwara Temple, which showcase exquisite Ganga and Chola architectural styles.

FAQ

Why is Kolar famous?

Kolar is historically famous for the Kolar Gold Fields (KGF), which were once among the deepest gold mines in the world, and for its ancient temples.

How far is Kolar from Bengaluru?

Kolar is approximately 70 km east of Bengaluru and is easily accessible via the well-maintained National Highway 75.

What is the best time to visit Kolar?

The best time to visit is from October to March when the weather is pleasant and cool, ideal for exploring temples and climbing Antaragange.

What are the main agricultural products of Kolar?

Kolar is famous for being a leading producer of tomatoes, milk, silk, and various varieties of mangoes in Karnataka.
