Mathura in one minute

Mathura — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Mathura is Krishna’s birthplace on the Yamuna — the Sri Krishna Janmabhoomi marks the sacred centre, Vishram Ghat is where the deity is said to have rested after Kansa, and the city’s ghats still carry the evening aarti. This was also a great art city: the Mathura school of sculpture cut red sandstone Buddhas and Yakshas through the Gupta age, and the museum keeps those stones.

Today the town blends temple tourism with the everyday economy: the giant IndianOil refinery on the Agra road (running since 1969), peda boilers and sweet factories, puja-garland trade, and paddy-and-mustard fields of the Braj countryside. Mathura Junction (MTJ) sits on the Delhi–Mumbai main line, Vrindavan is 11 km east, Goverdhan and Barsana make day trips, and Delhi lies ~150 km north on the expressway.

FAQ

Why is Mathura famous?

As Krishna’s birthplace — the Sri Krishna Janmabhoomi, Vishram Ghat, nearby Vrindavan, and the Mathura peda.

What is the population of Mathura?

As per Census 2011, Mathura city has 4,41,386 residents and the district 15,94,323.

What are the places to visit there?

Janmabhoomi, Vishram Ghat, Gita Mandir and the Government Museum — with Vrindavan, Goverdhan and Barsana close.

How do I reach Mathura?

Mathura Jn (MTJ) on the Delhi–Mumbai main line, Agra airport (AGR) ~60 km, and the expressway — Delhi ~150 km.
