Gangtok in one minute

Gangtok — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Gangtok rose to prominence as a major stop on the trade route between Tibet and British India, eventually becoming the capital of the Kingdom of Sikkim. Today, it serves as the administrative seat of the state, characterized by its steep hillsides, clean mountain air, and the presence of the Enchey Monastery. The city is governed by the Gangtok Municipal Corporation and is a hub for tourism and high-altitude research.

The Namgyal Institute of Tibetology and the nearby Rumtek Monastery establish the city as a globally recognized center for Tibetan Buddhism. The economy is driven by hospitality, organic agriculture, and traditional crafts. Located along the Rani Khola river valley, the city offers panoramic views of the Kanchenjunga range, maintaining a strict ban on plastic and smoking in public areas to preserve its ecological heritage.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Gangtok?

The ideal months are March to May for spring blooms and October to mid-December for clear views of the Kanchenjunga range. Monsoon months usually bring heavy rains and landslides.

Do I need a permit for Gangtok?

Indian tourists do not need permits for Gangtok city, but Protected Area Permits are mandatory for Nathu La, Tsomgo Lake, and North Sikkim, obtainable through registered travel agents.

How to reach Gangtok from the nearest airport?

Pakyong Airport is the closest but has limited flights. Most travelers fly to Bagdogra in West Bengal and take a 4—5 hour drive via NH10 through the Teesta valley.

Is plastic allowed in Gangtok?

Sikkim has a strict ban on single-use plastics and packaged drinking water bottles in many areas. Visitors are encouraged to use filtered water and carry reusable bags.
