Jamshedpur in one minute

Jamshedpur — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Jamshedpur — the Steel City — is India’s first planned industrial city, raised from the Sakchi village Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata chose for his steel dream; the 1919 works became Tata Steel, Asia’s first integrated private steel plant. Census 2011 counted about 6.3 lakh in the notified area, a UA of 13.4 lakh with Mango and Adityapur across the Swarnarekha.

The city runs on a grid of green — Jubilee Park’s fountains and rose gardens, Dimna Lake under the Dalma hills, and the Tata Football Academy’s grounds — while Tata Motors’ plant, XLRI’s management campus and the JRD Tata sports complex give the works-town a university-and-sport edge. Few Indian cities are run so quietly by corporate civic care.

FAQ

What is Jamshedpur famous for?

India’s first planned industrial city — Tata Steel (Asia’s first private steel plant, 1919), Jubilee Park, Dimna Lake and the Tata Football Academy.

What is the population of Jamshedpur?

Census 2011: about 6,31,364 in the notified area (queue figure 6,31,053, pending reconciliation); UA 13,39,438 with Mango and Adityapur; East Singhbhum 23,39,023.

What is the pincode of Jamshedpur?

The city starts at 831001; Jamshedpur spans 831001–832414 across Bistupur, Sakchi, Mango and Adityapur.

Which places should I visit there?

Jubilee Park’s fountains, the Tata Steel heritage belt, Dimna Lake — with the Dalma sanctuary as the wild day run.
