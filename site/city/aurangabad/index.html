Aurangabad in one minute

Aurangabad — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Aurangabad — officially Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar since 2023 — was raised by Malik Ambar (1610) and crowned by Aurangzeb’s vice-royalty: a walled city of fifty-two gates, the Bibi ka Maqbara marble tomb (the Taj of the Deccan) and the Panchakki water-mill that ran on a 6-km channel. Census 2011 counted 12.6 lakh in the city.

The district holds two UNESCO giants — Ajanta’s 2nd-century-BC Buddhist frescoes and Ellora’s monolithic Kailasa temple — plus Daulatabad’s unconquered fort and Paithan’s famous saris. The modern city adds the Chikalthana MIDC — auto plants, pharma and the wine presses of the Deccan valley.

FAQ

What is Aurangabad famous for?

The UNESCO caves of Ajanta and Ellora (the monolithic Kailasa temple), Bibi ka Maqbara — the Taj of the Deccan — Daulatabad fort, Paithani saris and the city of fifty-two gates.

What is the population of Aurangabad?

Census 2011: 12,63,808 in the city and 11,93,167 in the 2011-bounds UA; the district (now Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) counted 37,05,075.

What is the pincode of Aurangabad?

The city starts at 431001; it spans 431001–431013 plus the MIDC and CIDCO belts.

Which places should I visit there?

Ellora’s Kailasa and Ajanta’s frescoes, Bibi ka Maqbara, Daulatabad fort and Paithan — with Lonar’s meteor-crater lake as the far run.
