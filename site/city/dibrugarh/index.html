Dibrugarh in one minute

Dibrugarh — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Dibrugarh serves as the commercial and educational nerve centre of Upper Assam, famously known as the Tea City of India. Situated on the south bank of the Brahmaputra, it manages vast tea production and distribution. The city underwent significant reconstruction after the 1950 Assam—Tibet earthquake, which altered the course of the river and submerged much of the original township, leading to the development of modern flood protection systems.

As a major logistics hub, the city connects the oil-rich districts of Digboi and Duliajan with the rest of the country via the Bogibeel Bridge. It houses the prestigious Dibrugarh University and is a vital transit point for those travelling to eastern Arunachal Pradesh. The local economy is driven by the tea industry, natural gas extraction, and the historic timber trade found along the river banks.

FAQ

Why is Dibrugarh called the Tea City?

It is surrounded by some of the most productive tea estates in the world and serves as a major processing and auction hub for the industry.

Is Dibrugarh safe from river erosion?

Since the 1950 earthquake, extensive embankments and the Bogibeel Bridge projects have improved flood management, though the river remains a powerful natural force.

How can I reach Dibrugarh?

The city is well—connected by its own airport at Mohanbari, the Rajdhani Express at DBRG station, and the NH15 highway via the Bogibeel Bridge.

What is the best time to visit?

Between October and March, when the weather is cool and dry, making it ideal for tea garden tours and river cruises.
