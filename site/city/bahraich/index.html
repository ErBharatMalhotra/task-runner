Bahraich in one minute

Bahraich — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Bahraich is an ancient city in the Terai region of northeastern Uttar Pradesh, serving as the administrative headquarters of its namesake district. The city is renowned for the Dargah of Sayyad Salar Masud, a significant Sufi shrine that attracts millions of pilgrims annually during the Jeth Mela. Historically, it is associated with the legendary King Suhaldev, who is celebrated for his local governance and defense of the region.

The geography of Bahraich is defined by its proximity to the Saryu River and the dense forests of the Indo-Nepal border area. As a major trade center for timber, pulses, and sugar, it connects the fertile plains of Awadh with the Himalayan foothills. The city serves as a gateway to the Katarniaghat Wildlife Sanctuary, preserving a unique ecosystem of swamps and grasslands home to tigers and gharials.

FAQ

What is Bahraich famous for?

Bahraich is most famous for the Dargah of Sayyad Salar Masud, a revered Sufi shrine, and its historical association with King Suhaldev, alongside its role as a timber and sugar hub.

How to reach Katarniaghat from Bahraich?

Katarniaghat Wildlife Sanctuary is approximately 86 km from Bahraich city and can be reached via private taxis or state buses heading toward Nanpara and the Nepal border.

Which river flows through Bahraich?

The Saryu River, also known as the Sarju, flows through the district and is vital for the region—s irrigation and religious rituals.

Is Bahraich near the Nepal border?

Yes, Bahraich is a border district. The town of Rupaidiha, about 55 km from the city center, serves as a major land customs station for entry into Nepal.
