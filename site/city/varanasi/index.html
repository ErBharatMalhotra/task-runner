Varanasi in one minute

The living city of Shiva — three thousand years of ghats, silk and unbroken ritual on the Ganga.

Varanasi (Kashi, Benares) is among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, cradled in a bend of the Ganga in eastern Uttar Pradesh. Its ghats step down to the river — from Dashashwamedh’s evening Ganga Aarti to Assi Ghat at sunrise — and the old city behind them is a maze of lanes that have sold Benaresi silk, brassware and glass bangles for generations.

The city is a religious capital (Kashi Vishwanath draws millions), an education hub (Banaras Hindu University, 1916) and a craft economy of silk weaving, embroidery and lacquer work. Across the river at Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon, the ASI museum holds the Ashokan pillar capital that became India’s national emblem. The Delhi–Howrah main line, Lal Bahadur Shastri airport (VNS) and NH-2/NH-56 keep the pilgrim traffic moving.

FAQ

Why is Varanasi famous?

For the ghats of the Ganga and the Kashi Vishwanath temple, Benaresi silk, and being one of the world’s oldest living cities — plus Sarnath, where Buddhism’s first sermon was preached.

What is the population of Varanasi?

As per Census 2011, Varanasi city has 11,98,491 residents; the urban agglomeration is 14,35,113 and the district 36,82,194.

What are the places to visit there?

Dashashwamedh and Assi ghats, Kashi Vishwanath temple, Sarnath (10 km), Ramnagar Fort and BHU’s New Vishwanath Temple.

How do I reach Varanasi?

Varanasi Junction (BSB) on the Delhi–Howrah main line, Lal Bahadur Shastri airport (VNS) ~25 km away, and NH-2/NH-56 road links.
