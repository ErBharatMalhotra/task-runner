Katihar in one minute

Katihar — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Katihar is a prominent city in the eastern part of Bihar, serving as the administrative headquarters of its namesake district. Historically part of the Purnia Division, the city evolved as a critical transport node during the British Raj due to its proximity to the borders of West Bengal and Bangladesh. It is situated on the plains between the Mahananda and Ganga rivers, facilitating its growth as a regional trade center for agricultural produce.

The city is widely recognized for its Katihar Junction, one of the largest and busiest railway stations in the Indian Railways network. Economic life in the region was traditionally anchored by jute manufacturing, which flourished alongside a vibrant local economy of small-scale industries and wholesale markets. Today, Katihar remains a melting pot of cultures, influenced by its geographical position at the intersection of Mithila and the Surjapuri-speaking belt.

FAQ

What is Katihar famous for?

Katihar is primarily known as one of the most important railway junctions in India, connecting Bihar to the Northeast. It is also historically significant for its jute mills and makhana production.

Which rivers flow through Katihar?

The district is bounded by the Ganges in the south and the Mahananda river flows through the city region, significantly influencing its geography and agriculture.

Is Katihar a safe city for travelers?

Katihar is a typical Tier-2 Indian city. It is generally safe for travelers, though standard precautions should be taken when navigating crowded railway station areas at night.

How can I reach Katihar?

The city is best reached by train via Katihar Junction, which has direct connectivity to Delhi, Kolkata, and Guwahati. The nearest commercial airport is at Bagdogra.
