Kochi in one minute

Kochi — live weather, population, hidden places and cost of living, auto-updated.

Kochi is a natural harbour the Arabian Sea keeps rearranging — the Periyar and Vembanad backwaters open onto it, and every trading power that wanted the spice coast left a layer: the Paradesi Synagogue (1568) and Jew Town in Mattancherry, Portuguese churches and the Dutch Palace, then British Willingdon Island. The Chinese fishing nets at the Fort Kochi shore are still lowered by counterweight teams at dusk.

Today it is Kerala’s commercial capital — Cochin port and shipbuilding at the Willingdon belt, seafood and spice exports through Mattancherry, Infopark and SmartCity offices up in Kakkanad, and a metro that runs across the lagoon. Backwater tourism (Alleppey, Kumarakom an hour away) and the island’s heritage hotels keep the service economy humming. Ernakulam Junction (ERS) is South India’s busiest rail knot, Cochin International (COK) sits ~30 km north on the Angamaly road, and NH-66 threads the whole coast.

FAQ

Why is Kochi famous?

For the Chinese fishing nets, Fort Kochi’s colonial layers and the 1568 Paradesi synagogue, its backwaters and as Kerala’s port capital.

What is the population of Kochi?

As per Census 2011, Kochi city has 6,02,046 residents; the urban agglomeration is 21,19,724 and the district 32,82,266.

What are the places to visit there?

Fort Kochi, Mattancherry Palace, Paradesi synagogue, Bolgatty Island and Marine Drive — with Alleppey and Kumarakom an hour out.

How do I reach Kochi?

Ernakulam Junction (ERS) on the South Indian main line, Cochin International airport (COK) ~30 km, and the Kochi Metro for the city.
